# Church Website Redesign Strategy

## Purpose

The goal of the redesign is to make the website more effective at reaching people outside the congregation while still preserving what makes Churches of Christ distinct.

We want the site to feel visitor-strategy driven like the strongest broader church websites, while remaining identity driven in the best sense: simple, biblical, Christ-centered, and rooted in the worship and practices of Churches of Christ.

The website should communicate two things at once:

- You can come here without knowing the rules.
- What you will find here is simple, biblical, Christ-centered worship.

## Core Positioning

A strong positioning statement for the church could be:

**A simple, sincere church family helping people follow Jesus.**

For the local area, we should speak directly to Fulshear, Katy, and the surrounding west Houston communities:

**A church family in the Fulshear-Katy area learning to follow Jesus together.**

Supporting language:

**Simple worship. Bible-centered teaching. Real community. You are welcome here, whether you have followed Jesus for years or are just beginning to ask questions.**

This allows the church to be warm and accessible without sounding generic. It also allows us to be clearly Church of Christ without sounding closed off or defensive.

## The Main Strategy

Lead with the visitor's questions first, then explain our identity as the answer.

Many Church of Christ websites begin with doctrine, worship style, leadership, history, or internal ministries. Those things matter, but they may not be the first questions a newcomer is asking.

A person in Fulshear, Katy, or the surrounding Houston area who is looking for a church may be asking:

- Will my family feel welcome?
- Will I know where to go?
- Will my kids be safe?
- Will I be judged?
- Is this church serious about the Bible?
- Is this place real, or is it a production?
- Can I belong here?

The homepage and visitor pages should answer those questions before asking the visitor to understand everything about us.

## Recommended Homepage Structure

### 1. First Screen

The first screen should immediately answer who we are, where we are, and what someone should do next.

Recommended headline:

**A church family in the Fulshear-Katy area learning to follow Jesus together.**

Recommended supporting text:

**Simple worship. Bible-centered teaching. Real community. You are welcome here, whether you have followed Jesus for years or are just beginning to ask questions.**

Primary buttons:

- Plan Your Visit
- Watch Online

Also include:

- Sunday worship time
- Address
- Get Directions button

### 2. What To Expect This Sunday

This section should reduce anxiety before a first visit.

It should answer:

- How long is the service?
- What should I wear?
- Where should I park?
- Where do my kids go?
- What happens during worship?
- Will I be asked to give money?
- Will I be singled out as a guest?

### 3. Why We Worship Simply

This is where Church of Christ identity becomes beautiful instead of defensive.

Recommended language:

**We sing together without instruments because we believe every voice matters. We share communion every Sunday because Jesus is at the center of our gathering. We teach from Scripture because we believe God still speaks through His Word.**

This explains distinctive practices in a way that feels inviting, clear, and spiritually meaningful.

### 4. For Your Family

This is especially important for Fulshear, Katy, and west Houston because many visitors will be families.

Include:

- Nursery information
- Children's classes or children's worship
- Youth ministry
- Safety and check-in process
- What parents should expect on a first visit
- Where families should enter

### 5. A Place To Grow

Show that the church is more than a Sunday service.

Include:

- Bible classes
- Small groups or connection groups
- Prayer
- Serving opportunities
- Local outreach
- Bible studies
- Youth and family ministry

### 6. New Here? Start Here

Give visitors a clear and simple next-step path.

Recommended path:

1. Visit Sunday.
2. Meet someone at the welcome area.
3. Join a Bible class or group.
4. Ask questions.
5. Grow at your pace.

## Tone And Language

Avoid language that sounds like:

**We are the true church and here are the reasons.**

Use language that sounds like:

**We are trying to be the kind of church we read about in Scripture: devoted to Jesus, shaped by the Bible, gathered around communion, singing together, praying together, and caring for one another.**

This keeps the identity of Churches of Christ while removing unnecessary walls for people who are new, unsure, or returning to faith.

## Explaining Church Of Christ Distinctives

Use "why" language, not "we don't" language.

### A Cappella Singing

Instead of:

**We do not use instruments.**

Say:

**We sing a cappella because worship is something the whole church does together. You do not need musical skill. Your voice belongs.**

### Weekly Communion

Instead of:

**We take communion every Sunday.**

Say:

**Every Sunday, we pause around the Lord's Supper to remember Jesus. For us, communion is not an occasional ritual; it is a weekly reminder that Christ is the center.**

### Independent Congregation

Instead of:

**We are not a denomination.**

Say:

**Churches of Christ are independent congregations seeking to follow the pattern and spirit of the New Testament church.**

### Biblical Authority

Instead of:

**We speak where the Bible speaks.**

Say:

**We want Scripture to shape what we believe, how we worship, and how we treat people.**

## Local Emphasis

The website should feel rooted in the actual community, not just generically "Houston area."

Use local language such as:

**Serving Fulshear, Katy, Brookshire, Simonton, Richmond, and west Houston families.**

The site should speak to:

- Families moving into the area
- People looking for a church home after relocation
- Parents wanting spiritual grounding for their children
- People tired of performance-heavy religion
- Retirees, young families, and multigenerational households
- A fast-growing area that still needs real community

Strong local message:

**In a fast-growing area, people still need something steady: Jesus, Scripture, and a church family that knows their name.**

## The Website Promise

The site should make this promise:

**You will know what to expect before you arrive, and when you arrive, you will find a church that is simple, biblical, warm, and real.**

That is the blend we are aiming for.

## Site Voice And Content Guide

This section should guide the tone of every page, not just the homepage.

The entire site should sound:

- Warm, but not vague.
- Biblical, but not argumentative.
- Clear, but not cold.
- Inviting, but not sales-like.
- Distinctly Church of Christ, but not defensive.
- Local and personal, not generic.

Every page should feel like it is written for someone who may be visiting, returning to church, new to Churches of Christ, or unsure what they believe.

### The Standard Question For Every Page

Before publishing any page, ask:

**Would this page help a first-time visitor feel more confident, more welcome, and more clear about who we are?**

If the answer is no, the page probably needs to be rewritten or reorganized.

### The Tone Formula

Use this pattern throughout the website:

1. Start with the visitor's question or need.
2. Give a clear and warm answer.
3. Explain the biblical reason or Church of Christ practice.
4. Offer a simple next step.

Example:

**Wondering what worship is like? Our Sunday gathering is simple and focused on Jesus. We sing together, pray, share communion, and hear a message from Scripture. You are welcome to participate as much or as little as you feel comfortable.**

### Words To Use Often

These words reinforce the desired tone:

- Welcome
- Simple
- Sincere
- Jesus
- Scripture
- Family
- Together
- Questions
- Belong
- Grow
- Follow Jesus
- Come as you are
- At your pace

### Words And Phrases To Use Carefully

These words may be accurate, but they can feel unclear or insider-focused if not explained:

- Placing membership
- Invitation
- Elders
- Bible class
- A cappella
- Lord's Supper
- Fellowship
- New Testament church
- Sound doctrine
- Restoration

When these words are used, explain them in plain language.

Example:

**We share the Lord's Supper, also called communion, every Sunday to remember Jesus' death and resurrection.**

### Preferred Page Pattern

For most pages, use this order:

1. Plain-language invitation.
2. What the visitor needs to know.
3. Why it matters spiritually.
4. What happens next.
5. Who to contact or where to go.

This keeps pages from becoming only announcements, explanations, or internal ministry descriptions.

### Page Review Checklist

Use this checklist before approving major pages:

- Is the main point clear in the first few seconds?
- Is the language understandable to someone new to church?
- Does the page answer a real visitor question?
- Does it preserve our Church of Christ identity with warmth?
- Does it avoid sounding defensive or comparative?
- Is there a clear next step?
- Are service times, location, or contact details easy to find when relevant?
- Does the page sound like real people, not an institution?

## Where This Should Live During The Redesign

This strategy should be kept in the project as the guiding brief for the redesign.

Recommended places to apply it:

1. **Website strategy document**  
   Keep this Markdown file as the central reference for elders, staff, volunteers, and anyone helping with the redesign.

2. **Homepage copy**  
   The homepage should model the tone for the rest of the site. If the homepage feels visitor-first and biblically rooted, the rest of the site can follow its lead.

3. **Plan Your Visit page**  
   This should be the clearest expression of the strategy. It should answer visitor anxiety with warmth and explain Church of Christ worship in plain language.

4. **Content review checklist**  
   Use the page review checklist above whenever writing or approving site content.

5. **Design system or component notes**  
   Buttons, section headings, cards, and calls to action should use the same language patterns: clear, warm, and action-oriented.

6. **Future contributor guidance**  
   If the redesign involves multiple people writing or editing pages, this tone guide should be shared before any copy is written.

## Visual Direction Guide

The visual design should support the same blend as the writing:

**Warm and visitor-friendly, while still simple, sincere, biblical, and grounded.**

The best church websites are not effective because they look flashy. They are effective because visitors can quickly understand where they are, what the church is like, and what to do next.

### Visual Goals

The website should feel:

- Warm
- Clear
- Local
- Trustworthy
- Family-friendly
- Spiritually grounded
- Easy to navigate
- Modern without feeling trendy
- Simple without feeling plain

It should not feel:

- Corporate
- Overproduced
- Generic
- Dark or heavy
- Like an event flyer
- Like a members-only bulletin
- Like a megachurch imitation

### What Makes The Best Church Sites Work Visually

The strongest church websites usually share these visual qualities:

1. **Clear first impression**  
   Within a few seconds, visitors can tell who the church is, where it is, when it meets, and how to visit.

2. **Real photography**  
   The best sites show real people, real worship, real families, real classrooms, real fellowship, and real spaces. This helps a visitor imagine themselves there.

3. **Simple navigation**  
   New visitors can quickly find Plan Your Visit, service times, location, kids, sermons, beliefs, and contact information.

4. **Strong visual hierarchy**  
   Important things look important. Service times, address, and visitor next steps should not compete with announcements or secondary content.

5. **White space and breathing room**  
   Good spacing makes the site feel calmer, clearer, and easier to trust.

6. **Mobile-first design**  
   Many first-time visitors will check the site from a phone, often while comparing churches. The mobile experience should make service time, location, directions, and Plan Your Visit easy to find.

7. **Consistent calls to action**  
   Buttons should be clear and repeated in helpful places: Plan Your Visit, Watch Online, Get Directions, Ask a Question, Request Prayer.

8. **A warm but restrained color palette**  
   The site should feel welcoming without relying on loud colors, heavy gradients, or overly trendy visual effects.

9. **Human-centered section design**  
   The site should prioritize people and pathways over blocks of text. Each section should help the visitor decide what to do next.

10. **Visual proof of church life**  
   Photos and page sections should show that the church is alive: worship, children, Bible study, prayer, service, fellowship, and community presence.

### Photography Guidance

Photography will matter greatly.

Use real photos whenever possible:

- People greeting each other
- Families arriving
- Worship from the visitor's point of view
- Bible classes
- Children's areas
- Youth activities
- Fellowship meals
- Prayer moments
- Service projects
- Exterior entrance and parking
- Welcome area or lobby

Avoid relying too heavily on:

- Stock photos
- Empty building photos
- Overly staged portraits
- Dark worship photos where faces are hard to see
- Cropped images that do not help visitors understand the place
- Announcement graphics as primary homepage visuals

The question for each image should be:

**Would this help a visitor picture themselves here?**

### Homepage Visual Priorities

The homepage should visually prioritize:

1. Plan Your Visit
2. Service time
3. Location and directions
4. What to expect
5. Kids and family information
6. Watch online
7. Church identity and beliefs
8. Next steps
9. Events and announcements

Events and announcements matter, but they should not visually dominate the homepage for first-time visitors.

### Layout Principles

Use clear page sections with one purpose each.

Good section examples:

- Hero: who we are and how to visit.
- Visit: service time, address, directions.
- Expect: worship, dress, kids, giving, communion.
- Family: nursery, children, youth, safety.
- Identity: simple worship, Scripture, communion, a cappella singing.
- Community: groups, classes, serving, prayer.
- Next Steps: visit, watch, ask, pray, study.

Avoid pages that feel like a long stream of unrelated blocks.

### Typography

The typography should be readable, warm, and steady.

Use:

- Large, clear headings
- Short paragraphs
- Easy-to-scan sections
- Generous line spacing
- Strong contrast between text and background

Avoid:

- Tiny text
- Long paragraphs
- Too many font styles
- Decorative fonts that reduce trust or readability
- All-caps headings used too often

### Color And Atmosphere

The color palette should feel local, warm, and grounded.

Good directions:

- Warm neutrals
- Soft whites
- Deep green, blue, or muted earth tones
- One strong accent color for buttons and key actions
- Natural colors that pair well with real photography

Avoid:

- Heavy dark themes
- Neon colors
- Overuse of beige or brown
- Purple/blue gradient-heavy design
- Visual effects that feel trendy but distract from clarity

### Visual Identity For Churches Of Christ

The visual design should reflect Church of Christ identity through simplicity and sincerity.

That means:

- Less stage-performance emphasis
- More congregation and family emphasis
- Less hype
- More Scripture, communion, prayer, and shared worship
- Less polished perfection
- More real, local church life

The site should feel like:

**This is a real church family centered on Jesus.**

Not:

**This is a religious brand trying to impress me.**

### Design Review Question

Before approving a design, ask:

**Does this design make it easier for a first-time visitor to trust us, understand us, and imagine showing up this Sunday?**

If the answer is no, the design may look good but still fail the mission.

## Top Five Practical Moves

1. Build the homepage around first-time visitors, not only members.
2. Add a rich Plan Your Visit page with parking, kids, worship flow, communion, singing, dress, giving, and service length.
3. Explain Church of Christ identity through warmth and purpose, not contrast and correction.
4. Use real photos of our people, lobby, worship space, kids areas, Bible classes, and fellowship.
5. Create clear next steps: Visit, Watch, Ask a Question, Request Prayer, Study the Bible, and Find Community.

## Final Summary

The goal is not to make the site look like a megachurch.

The goal is to make it feel clear, safe, sincere, and deeply rooted.

A visitor should leave the website thinking:

**I know what this church is about, and I would not feel weird showing up there.**
